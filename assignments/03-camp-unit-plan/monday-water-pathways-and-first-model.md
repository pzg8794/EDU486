## Non-Negotiable Evidence And Access Rules

1. **Begin with youth thinking from Friday.** Do not begin by announcing the pathway.
2. **Give the challenge, wait, then follow up.** Let scholars try, revise, and make mistakes before offering a hint. Use "What do you notice?", "What makes you think that?", and "What do we still need to know?"
3. **One direction at a time; whole sequence visible.** Youth may talk, point, draw, write, move a card, photograph, work with a partner, use a seated/quiet route, or pass and return.
4. **No speed competition.** The timer protects the schedule; it does not rank thinking, reading, or stamina.
5. **Do not force body, illness, food, family, or identity disclosure.** An environment-only example is always available.
6. **Correct the scale claim.** Microplastics are plastic particles smaller than 5 millimeters. Some are visible; others require magnification. They are not all invisible.
7. **Name the model.** Cut paper and moving paper pieces are safe proxies for thinking about scale and transport. They are not microplastic evidence.
8. **Detection is not proof of route or harm.** A suspected particle is not chemically confirmed plastic. Finding plastic in a sample or organism does not, by itself, prove how it arrived or what effect it caused.
9. **Fish/KOH work is not Monday evidence.** Potassium hydroxide is instructor-controlled. Campers do not handle it. Preparing fish tissue on Monday begins a multi-day process; it does not reveal microplastics that day.
10. **Build the checklist from youth learning.** Begin with a blank Gotta-Have Checklist. After each investigation ask, "What did we learn from this that should go on our checklist when we revise our model?" Record scholars' words without replacing them with an adult conclusion.
11. **Preserve the close.** If time runs short, shorten the fish block and let adults finish preparation after youth leave. Do not delete the first-draft model or exit question.

### 3. AASTHA'S IMPOSSIBLE CUT - 9:32-9:47

Give the challenge first and let the scholars, Aastha, and Piter try before offering steps or hints. Use one shared set of paper, measuring tools, and scissors:

1. Show or measure **1 meter**, **10 centimeters**, **1 centimeter**, **5 millimeters**, and **1 millimeter**.
2. Ask scholars to choose measuring, holding, cutting, labeling, checking, observing, or pass-and-return roles.
3. Ask whether classroom tools can make and verify a **1 micrometer** strip. Wait while the group tests ideas; do not fix the challenge too quickly or ask youth to keep cutting after the tool limit is clear.
4. Place **AGREE**, **NOT SURE**, and **DISAGREE** placards along a line, with room to show stronger or weaker positions. Show the intentionally inaccurate claim **"All microplastics are invisible."** Standing on the line is one route. Pointing to or holding a placard while seated, asking a partner to place a marker, or passing and returning are equally valid. Ask what evidence would help the group decide.
5. Use the scale evidence and short source to revise the claim: **microplastics are smaller than 5 millimeters; some are visible and some require magnification.**
6. Ask, "What becomes harder to measure or notice as the scale gets smaller?"

The purpose is proportional reasoning, claim revision, and tool limits, not hand skill, speed, or a perfect strip. The false sentence is a claim to test, never an answer to copy.

### 4. AASTHA'S ESCAPE THE WIND - 9:47-10:00

Open the [Monday teaching slides](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1nHcWOyRAVQDjdqDlpf37868jD1PIu8lhNMMJPQfZ24s/edit) to the **Moving-Air Transport Model**. Place exactly **12 lightweight paper pieces** inside the same taped starting box and mark one finish line. Say:

> "These paper pieces are a model for lightweight fragments. Move at least half across the boundary without touching them with your hands."

Let scholars investigate with cardboard, a clipboard, a notebook, or a paper fan. A breath route may be available only if a scholar independently prefers it and instructors approve; it is never required. Give each person one equal-length turn. Record the starting count, how many pieces crossed the finish line, the direction they moved, and where pieces stopped. Count pieces that did not move too. This is the **transport pattern**. Count all 12 pieces again during cleanup and say:

> "Moving air transported our paper model. This supports a mechanism we can investigate; it does not prove that these pieces are plastic or explain every environmental pathway."

Ask, "If moving air can carry lightweight pieces, what might moving water do?"

### 7. BEFORE / DURING / AFTER CONSENSUS MODEL - 10:20-10:45

Begin by comparing models: the cut paper shows scale limits, moving paper shows one transport mechanism, and a globe can show relative locations while leaving out stars and exact travel distances. **Every model is an approximation that shows some things and leaves others out.**

Piter facilitates one shared first draft; Aastha records exact youth language and watches access. Build the model in three visible bands:

1. **BEFORE - SOURCE + RELEASE:** What product or material contained plastic? How could smaller pieces or fibers leave it?
2. **DURING - MOVE + ENCOUNTER:** How could air, water, soil, or food transport it? What living thing might encounter it?
3. **AFTER - EVIDENCE + UNKNOWN + FAIR ACTION:** What supports an arrow? What remains possible or unknown? Who can reduce the pathway or share responsibility?

Use Aastha's optional shared scaffold orally or on moveable cards: **WE THINK... / WE NOTICED... / WE WONDER... / WE MAY CHANGE....** Do not print an individual worksheet and do not judge first-draft ideas as right or wrong.

Return to G2-L1, G2-L2, the scale strips, wind model, video, and Wonder Wall. Invite each person to add one idea or question. Standing to place a card is available, not required; pointing, placing from a seated position, drawing, dictating, photographing, partnering, passing, and returning are equally scientific routes. Use **OBSERVATION / POSSIBLE MEANING / QUESTION** labels only to clarify the kind of idea, not to grade it. Save **SUPPORTED / POSSIBLE / UNKNOWN** for later revisions after the group has more evidence.

Ask after the model: **"What did we learn today that should go on our blank Gotta-Have Checklist when we revise this model?"** Add scholars' words or pictures.

Offer one justice question without requiring a complete answer:

> **Who makes decisions about products, drains, wastewater, beach maintenance, or monitoring, and who should help change the pathway?**

Do not ask youth to map private homes or disclose family practices.

### 8. FISH DISSECTION + PREPARATION - 10:45-11:15

April confirmed the fish, KOH, small jars, dissecting tools, and trays. Sand
saltwater separation begins Tuesday; recovery follows Wednesday after the
sample has had a day to settle.

1. Show the intact fish or a clear photograph. Ask, "What can we observe before anything is cut?"
2. Separate **observation** from **inference** on two sticky notes or cards.
3. Offer equivalent roles: supervised cutter, close observer, image observer, diagrammer, photographer, question recorder, materials monitor, seated coordinator, cleanup planner, or pass-and-return.
4. Invite scholars to cut with the approved tools under direct adult supervision. No scholar must touch or cut the fish, and facilitators pause the cutting route if the setup is not safe.
5. Place the agreed tissue portions in the pre-labeled small jars. Record fish/sample ID, tissue type, date, and preparer.
6. Adults seal and retain the jars for instructor-controlled KOH preparation. Campers never handle or add KOH.
7. Stop by 11:15 even if adults must finish preparation later.

**Evidence boundary:** Monday documents external anatomy, tissue selection, and
the beginning of a method. It does **not** show that the fish contains
microplastics, establish an exposure route, or prove a health effect.
