**Monday success looks like this:** Youth use Friday's observations, Aastha's scale and wind investigations, and one short source to build a **before -> during -> after** consensus model of one possible **source -> release -> movement -> encounter** pathway. ## Non-Negotiable Evidence And Access Rules

1. **Begin with youth thinking from Friday.** Do not begin by announcing the pathway.
2. **Ask, wait, then follow up.** Use "What do you notice?", "What makes you think that?", and "What do we still need to know?"
3. **One direction at a time; whole sequence visible.** Youth may talk, point, draw, write, move a card, photograph, work with a partner, use a seated/quiet route, or pass and return.
4. **No speed competition.** The timer protects the schedule; it does not rank thinking, reading, or stamina.
5. **Do not force body, illness, food, family, or identity disclosure.** An environment-only example is always available.
6. **Correct the scale claim.** Microplastics are plastic particles smaller than 5 millimeters. Some are visible; others require magnification. They are not all invisible.
7. **Name the model.** Cut paper and moving paper pieces are safe proxies for thinking about scale and transport. They are not microplastic evidence.
8. **Detection is not proof of route or harm.** A suspected particle is not chemically confirmed plastic. Finding plastic in a sample or organism does not, by itself, prove how it arrived or what effect it caused.
9. **Fish/KOH work is not Monday evidence.** Potassium hydroxide is instructor-controlled. Campers do not handle it. Preparing fish tissue on Monday begins a multi-day process; it does not reveal microplastics that day.
10. **Preserve the close.** If time runs short, shorten the fish block and let adults finish preparation after youth leave. Do not delete the consensus-model revision or exit question.

### 3. AASTHA'S IMPOSSIBLE CUT - 9:32-9:47

Give one direction at a time. Use one shared set of paper, measuring tools, and scissors:

1. Show or measure **1 meter**, **10 centimeters**, **1 centimeter**, **5 millimeters**, and **1 millimeter**.
2. Ask scholars to choose measuring, holding, cutting, labeling, checking, observing, or pass-and-return roles.
3. Ask whether classroom tools can make and verify a **1 micrometer** strip. Do not ask youth to keep cutting until they fail.
4. Show the intentionally inaccurate claim **"All microplastics are invisible."** Scholars may choose **AGREE / DISAGREE / NOT SURE**, explain through any response route, or pass and return. Ask what evidence would help the group decide.
5. Use the scale evidence and short source to revise the claim: **microplastics are smaller than 5 millimeters; some are visible and some require magnification.**
6. Ask, "What becomes harder to measure or notice as the scale gets smaller?"

The purpose is proportional reasoning, claim revision, and tool limits, not hand skill, speed, or a perfect strip. The false sentence is a claim to test, never an answer to copy.

### 4. AASTHA'S ESCAPE THE WIND - 9:47-10:00

Place a small, countable set of lightweight paper pieces inside a tray or taped boundary. Say:

> "These paper pieces are a model for lightweight fragments. Move at least half across the boundary without touching them with your hands."

Let scholars investigate with cardboard, a clipboard, a notebook, or a paper fan. A breath route may be available only if a scholar independently prefers it and instructors approve; it is never required. Stop after the transport pattern is visible, count every piece during cleanup, and say:

> "Moving air transported our paper model. This supports a mechanism we can investigate; it does not prove that these pieces are plastic or explain every environmental pathway."

Ask, "If moving air can carry lightweight pieces, what might moving water do?"

### 7. BEFORE / DURING / AFTER CONSENSUS MODEL - 10:20-10:45

Piter facilitates one shared model; Aastha records exact youth language and watches access. Build the model in three visible bands:

1. **BEFORE - SOURCE + RELEASE:** What product or material contained plastic? How could smaller pieces or fibers leave it?
2. **DURING - MOVE + ENCOUNTER:** How could air, water, soil, or food transport it? What living thing might encounter it?
3. **AFTER - EVIDENCE + UNKNOWN + FAIR ACTION:** What supports an arrow? What remains possible or unknown? Who can reduce the pathway or share responsibility?

Use Aastha's optional shared scaffold orally or on moveable cards: **CLAIM - We think... / EVIDENCE - We noticed... / LIMIT - We still need... / CHANGE - We would revise....** Do not print an individual worksheet.

Return to G2-L1, G2-L2, the scale strips, wind model, video, and Wonder Wall. Place **SUPPORTED**, **POSSIBLE**, or **UNKNOWN** beside each relationship. A scholar can add science by pointing, placing a card, drawing an arrow, dictating, photographing, asking a question, or protecting an uncertainty.

Add one justice question:

> **Who makes decisions about products, drains, wastewater, beach maintenance, or monitoring, and who should help change the pathway?**

Do not ask youth to map private homes or disclose family practices.
