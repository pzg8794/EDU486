## Five Day 3 Missions

| Mission | What scholars did | Evidence language |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Sand Detectives** | Sieved the saved Day 1 sand by particle size, added salt water, and observed separation and settling. | Sieving sorts by size. Density separation and settling can help materials separate; visual inspection does not confirm plastic. |
| **Litter Meter Mission** | Used measuring wheels and recorded at least 51 visible litter observations across roughly 400 meters. | This is a rough field rate of about 0.13 observations per meter, or about one every 8 meters. Not every item was confirmed plastic. |
| **Tire-Wear Test Ride** | Experienced bicycle motion, discussed friction and wear, collected visible residue, and examined it with magnification. | The activity produced visible residue. It did not chemically confirm tire polymer or microplastic identity. |
| **Toxicology Lab Quest** | Joined the camp-wide visiting-scientist synthetic-tissue filtering model and compared results. | The model supported questions about detection and evidence. It did not prove a human exposure route or health effect. |
| **My Scientist Identity** | Used identity beads to recognize investigating, designing, connecting, caring, revising, and advocating. | Beads name complementary ways of participating in science; they do not rank scholars or assign a fixed identity. |

## Access-To-Agency Record

The evidence includes measuring, observing, counting, riding, walking,
recording, photographing, using tools, comparing, drawing, listening, speaking,
working with a partner, and stepping back and returning. No single role owns
the science. The souvenir and recap prompts allow scholars to point, speak,
draw, demonstrate, use a partner, or pass and return.

The evidence does **not** establish a diagnosis, identity category, motivation,
or complete understanding. Quiet participation and absence from a recording
are not treated as absence of thinking.

## Evidence Boundaries

- A visible particle or residue is not automatically confirmed plastic.
- A field count is an observation record, not a complete pollution survey.
- A teaching model can make a mechanism discussable without proving that the
  modeled event occurred in a particular body.
- Automatic transcripts preserve uncertain wording and require source-audio
  review before an exact quotation is publicly attributed.
- Touched images improve exposure, contrast, orientation, and instructional
  legibility while preserving the real camp activity.
- The five-mission story visual is a designed interpretation; original
  photographs and videos remain the primary record.
