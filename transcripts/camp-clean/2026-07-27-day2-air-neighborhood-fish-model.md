## 1. Moving Air and Particle Transport

**Approximate time:** 9:54-9:58 a.m.
**Activity:** Youth observed how moving air affected lightweight pieces and
used the observation to begin thinking about transport.

**Facilitator, video 01 at 00:00**
> See how the smallest pieces are moving faster? If you keep blowing, you see
> how this plastic moves.

**Evidence note:** The activity modeled moving air and relative particle
motion. It did not measure environmental airborne microplastic or prove that
every classroom particle was plastic.

**Approximate time:** 10:29-10:38 a.m.

**Youth voice, closing model at 00:35**

**Youth voice, closing model at 00:56**
> Then the trash can appears to prevent microplastics [from reaching] the
> water and the lake.

**Youth voice, closing model at 01:25**

**Evidence note:** Statements about illness, death, or a route into a body were
emerging model ideas. The activity supported causal questioning and revision;
it did not prove a human health effect or a specific exposure route.

## 3. Movement and Relationship Reset

**Approximate time:** 10:50 a.m.
**Activity:** The racket-and-ball routine gave youth a movement break while
representing how force, contact, and relationships can redirect an object.

**Clean record:** The available recording documents the movement setup more
clearly than the spoken explanation. The activity was used as an embodied
connection, not as direct evidence that a microplastic follows the same path
as the ball.

## 4. Neighborhood and Pathway Maps

**Approximate time:** 11:06 a.m.
**Activity:** Youth drew or mapped places they knew, noticed possible plastic
pathways, and considered what a community decision-maker could change.

**Youth questions preserved from the session transcript**
> How do microplastics get on the beach?
>
> How does plastic transfer to other stuff?
>
> How quickly does plastic move through the ocean?
>
> How can we help minimize trash spread?

**Youth voice**
> [My neighborhood is] mostly clean, but there are some small pieces of trash.

**Facilitator**
> You can write, draw, or make a mind map.

**Evidence note:** Youth could use a familiar place, a public place, or an
invented example. No one was required to disclose a home address or private
family information.

## What Was Removed

- projector setup, coaching alignment, and post-camp team debriefs;
- repeated fish-cutting directions and unrelated room conversation;
- names and health or family disclosures;
- the incorrect expansion of KOH; and
- unsupported claims presented as conclusions rather than model hypotheses.
