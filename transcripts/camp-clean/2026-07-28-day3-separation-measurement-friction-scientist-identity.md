## 1. Sand Sieving and Saltwater Density Separation

**Approximate time:** 9:33-9:46 a.m.
**Activity:** Youth used stacked sieves to separate particles by size, added
saltwater to examine density-based separation, and left the mixture to settle.

**Youth A, video 08 at 00:06**
> We put in sand that we collected from day one. We used a - not a grinder,
> but like a...

**Facilitator**
> Filter.

**Youth A**
> Filter. Then we put water in it. We're going to let it sit and see what's in
> it.

**Youth B, video 08 at 01:35**
> We got sand last week, and we were [sieving] it to get the sand particles
> out.

**Evidence note:** A sieve separates by particle size. Saltwater can support a
density-separation investigation, but a floating or retained particle is only
suspected plastic until its material is confirmed.

## 4. Visiting Scientist: Synthetic-Tissue Model

**Approximate time:** 11:06-11:46 a.m.
**Activity:** A visiting toxicologist introduced a synthetic "brain" model and
demonstrated careful filtration before youth participated.

**Guest scientist, video 11 at 00:00**
> I also work here, and I'm also a Warner student. Today we're going to be
> dissolving a brain. For our purposes, it's a brain, but in actuality it is
> synthetic, so we made it.

**Guest scientist, video 11 at 00:25**
> I want everybody first to check out how to filter the correct way, to make
> sure that there aren't gaps.

**Evidence note:** This was a teaching model for filtration and toxicology
questions. A synthetic material is not a human brain, and the model did not
prove a health effect in people.

## 5. Identi-Beads: Recognizing Ways of Doing Science

**Approximate time:** 11:47 a.m.-12:08 p.m.
**Activity:** Youth considered identities demonstrated through their work and
selected beads representing contributions they recognized in themselves.

**Facilitator, video 14 at 00:00**
> Environmental advocate: if you're out here fighting for the environment,
> telling people about microplastics and pollution, this might be something
> you want to protect.

**Facilitator, video 14 at 00:47**
> [An altruist] is somebody who is thinking about other people and volunteering
> to help them.

**Facilitator, video 14 at 01:04**
> Scientists sometimes study things that are controversial. They have to be
> brave and keep going with the research.

**Identity choices used in the activity:** designer, environmental advocate,
altruist, brave scientist, motivator, investigator, inventor, storyteller, and
another self-described contribution.

**Evidence note:** A bead recognized a contribution from that day. It did not
rank youth, diagnose a trait, or permanently define anyone's identity.
