## 3. Mayor and Stakeholder Policy Role-Play

**Activity:** A youth mayor considered three policy approaches while
participants argued from stakeholder roles and challenged one another's
reasoning.

**Facilitator**
> The mayor is making a decision. The mayor has to pick one of these policies.

**Policy A**
> Install stormwater capture systems.

**Policy B**
> Require plastic-packaging companies to fund recycling, cleanup, and more
> sustainable packaging through producer-responsibility rules.

**Policy C**
> Use education, fishing-gear recycling, and community cleanup initiatives.

**Parks stakeholder, 36:56**
> I clean the parks every day. There's a lot of plastic and garbage. After
> storms, I always clean up plastic and fishing line.

**Tourism stakeholder, 41:40**
> The mayor should provide more garbage and recycling cans, especially in
> tourist areas, and use campaigns that educate the public.

**Tourism stakeholder, 42:10**
> Policy B cannot be properly implemented without C, and it cannot be advanced
> without A. B needs C to educate the public and A to advance the tools.

**Restaurant stakeholder, 48:30**
> We should not blame only the person called a "litterbug." A lot of pollution
> comes from industrial processes or improper disposal. Producers can fund
> collection, sorting, and processing.

**Environmental-scientist stakeholder, 51:04**
> People need to be more educated and care more. Capture technology can also
> help control microplastics in water.

**Evidence note:** The discussion supported a combined approach instead of
placing all responsibility on individuals. The automatic transcripts conflict
about which policy letter won the final close vote, so this clean record does
not claim a verified winner.

## 5. Wearable Advocacy Art

**Activity:** Youth reused materials to create wearable public messages about
what they cared about and what they wanted others to do.

**Facilitator, video 04 at 00:06**
> Any ideas or inspiration? What do you care about? Animals? The environment?
> Brainstorm.

**Facilitator, session transcript**
> You need a slogan - words that say why the person looking at your clothing
> should do something to change the environment. You also need an image. It can
> be fabric, something you draw, or a symbol.

**Youth-created message**
> Protect the seas, protect your home.

**Youth-created message**
> Save the ocean, save your heart.

**Youth-created interpretation**
> Nature wrapped around plastic.

**Evidence note:** The artifacts connected science learning with agency and
public communication. Youth selected their own message and form; no one had to
disclose a private experience.
