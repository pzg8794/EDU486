# Thursday, July 30 - Youth-Led Showcase

**Included recording window:** Camp and showcase activity before 1:00 p.m. EDT
**Source boundary:** A direct July 30 iPhone inventory confirmed 10 camp
photographs from 12:02-12:35 p.m. and no Day 5 videos. This clean record uses
same-session automatic recorder transcripts. Their timestamps are elapsed
recording times, not clock times. Two files created after 6:00 p.m. were
quarantined outside the camp record.

## 1. Preparing the Sand-Sampling Station

**Activity:** Youth practiced collecting a representative shallow sample,
labeling it, and using three sieves before teaching visitors.

**Facilitator, workshop transcript at 08:51**
> Go about half an inch deep, pick it up, and pour it. Take a sample from the
> entire surface inside the red square.

**Facilitator, 10:00**
> The problem was that you took the sample from the same spot. You were
> supposed to take from different spots.

**Facilitator, 12:00**
> Once visitors collect their sand sample, tell them to label it with the tape.
> You have to use the three sieves together.

**Evidence note:** This was a demonstration of systematic sampling and size
separation. The showcase sand did not recreate exact beach conditions or
confirm that retained particles were plastic.

## 2. Preparing to Lead

**Instructor, workshop transcript at 51:02**
> Today is your day. You are servant leaders. Serving means helping others

> championing, and helping the younger scholars grow.

**Instructor, 51:55**

## 3. Telling the Whole Investigation Story

**Facilitator, workshop transcript at 48:00**
> This table tells the story of the experiments, results, and everything we did
> to answer: How does plastic break into tiny pieces, move through the
> environment and into living bodies, and how can we stop it fairly?

**Youth voice, education transcript at 04:43**
> If people don't have a trash can, they may throw trash in the lake. It can
> get to the fish. We wanted to change the environment by putting trash cans
> there so people wouldn't have to litter.

**Evidence note:** The model included emerging explanations about animals and
people. Those relationships helped organize questions; they did not by
themselves prove a health outcome.

**Visitor**

**Facilitator**

**Youth voice, workshop transcript at 01:17:27**
> We went outside and measured the distance to see how much plastic or litter
> was in the streets. We walked about 400 meters, and we seen 51 pieces of

**Evidence note:** The original route record was about 1,331 feet, which is
about 406 meters. The group documented 51 or more visible litter observations.
Not every item was confirmed as plastic.

study; it does not chemically confirm plastic or prove harm.
