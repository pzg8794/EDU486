# Friday, July 31 - Youth Retrospective and Future Design

**Included recording window:** Final camp reflection before the university
poster session
**Primary source:** Same-session automatic recorder transcript
**Source boundary:** This clean record preserves actionable youth feedback and
adult prompts while removing names, food and transportation logistics,
incidental personal conversation, and unrelated event setup.

## 1. More Time and More Hands-On Investigation

Youth identified several priorities for a future camp:

- more time using microscopes;
- more hands-on activities, especially constructing things;
- less rushing during investigation; and
- more opportunities for physical activity.

**Evidence note:** These requests are direct design data. They do not imply
that every participant preferred the same activity or pace.

## 2. Sensory Feedback About the Fish Activity

When facilitators asked whether one activity should be changed or removed,
some youth identified the fish activity. Follow-up discussion connected the
concern to smell and texture. A mask was suggested as one possible support,
but the discussion did not establish that a mask would make the activity
acceptable for everyone.

**Access note:** Offering equipment is not the same as obtaining consent.
Future planning should include advance information, a non-fish route to the
same scientific idea, and permission to decline without losing participation
or belonging.

## 3. Multiple Ways to Give Feedback

Facilitators invited youth to speak, write with markers, or use paper so they
could contribute without relying on one public response mode. The group used
pluses for what should continue and arrows for what should be revised rather
than classifying an entire experience as simply good or bad.

**Evidence note:** The plus-and-arrow structure supported revision language,
but adult prompts still shaped the categories and pace of the conversation.
Silence cannot be interpreted as agreement.

## 4. Making the Showcase Route Visible

Youth suggested numbering showcase stations so visitors could understand
where to begin and how to move through the experience.

**Design implication:** A visible route can support both youth presenters and
visitors without requiring everyone to memorize or verbally explain the full
sequence.

## 5. Future Questions and Topics

Youth proposed future investigations involving friction and vehicles, air and
water pollution, physical activity, and social issues they wanted to change.
The conversation connected feedback about the current camp to possible future
curriculum rather than treating youth only as evaluators of an adult plan.

## 6. Evidence Boundary Around Empowerment

Near the close, adults asked whether participants felt more empowered. Because
the wording introduced the desired interpretation and follow-up responses were
brief, this record does not use that exchange as independent evidence that the
camp caused empowerment. Stronger evidence would ask an open question, allow
private or delayed responses, and document how youth describe any change in
their own words.

## What Was Removed

- youth names and personal future plans;
- food, cleanup, transportation, and equipment logistics;
- unrelated university poster-session setup;
- repeated crowd-direction language; and
- adult praise that did not add distinct evidence about youth learning or
  preference.
