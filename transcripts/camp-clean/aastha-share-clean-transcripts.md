## Cross-Day Youth Story

### Notice and Question

On Friday, youth chose investigation locations, compared predictions with
observations, used magnification, and collected labeled samples. One youth
explained the purpose as:

> To have research and educate.

Another compared an estimate of 89 with a count of 40-something:

> I was wrong. But I knew about the tiny plastics.

### Connect Systems

On Monday, youth used air, neighborhood, fish, and shared-model activities to
connect sources and pathways. Their questions included:

> How do microplastics get on the beach?
>
> How does plastic transfer to other stuff?
>
> How can we help minimize trash spread?

A youth model explanation connected air and water:

> The air makes it also land in the water.

### Measure and Test

On Tuesday, youth separated sand, recorded 51 or more visible litter
observations across about 1,331 feet, examined tire-wear residue, learned from
a visiting toxicologist, and recognized scientific identities.

Youth A summarized:

> We used sand from day one, filtered it, added water, used the measuring
> wheel, counted trash, rode the bicycle, and saw friction. Honestly, it was
> fun.

Youth B summarized:

> We filtered the sand, found 51 plus, scratched the tire on sandpaper, and
> looked at the particles with the microscope. It was a fun experiment.

### Revise, Debate, and Advocate

On Wednesday, youth revised their model with evidence from prior activities,
debated who should carry responsibility, voted, and created wearable advocacy
art. The policy discussion moved beyond blaming individuals and toward
education, infrastructure, and producer responsibility.

Youth-created messages included:

> Protect the seas, protect your home.
>
> Save the ocean, save your heart.
>
> Nature wrapped around plastic.

### Lead and Teach

On Thursday, youth led stations and explained their evidence to visitors.
Their public solutions included more trash and recycling cans, especially at
the beginning, middle, end, and pier.

A youth explained:

> We walked about 400 meters, and we seen 51 pieces of plastic and trash.

The clean evidence note preserves the youth's wording while clarifying that the
record was 51 or more **visible litter observations**, not 51 chemically
confirmed plastic particles.

## Important Limits

- Speaker letters are activity-specific and are not youth names.
- Quotes remain tied to timestamps in the daily records.
- Suspected particles were not always chemically confirmed.
- Models helped youth reason; they did not prove a human exposure route or
  health effect.
- Only camp material from 7:15 a.m.-1:00 p.m. is included.
