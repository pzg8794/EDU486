## What Youth Did Across The Week

Youth moved from prediction and field collection to model building,
measurement, evidence revision, policy reasoning, advocacy, public teaching,
and critique of the camp itself. Their questions and actions included choosing
sampling sites, comparing estimates with counts, mapping possible pathways,
correcting a doubled measuring route, revising a shared model, arguing across
stakeholder roles, making public messages, leading showcase stations, and
proposing changes for a future camp.

The final retrospective identified more microscope and building time, less
rushing, more movement, a non-fish route to the same scientific idea, and a
numbered showcase path. These are design data for future teaching, not proof
that every participant shared the same preference.

## Evidence Limits

- Speaker-role labels are anonymous and activity-specific. They are not youth
  names or stable identities across days.
- Photographs and recordings show visible participation and artifacts; they do
  not prove what a learner understood, felt, or chose privately.
- Visible litter, retained sand particles, and magnified tire residue were not
  always chemically confirmed as plastic.
- Models supported explanation and revision; they did not prove a human
  exposure route or health effect.
- Day 5 has 10 source photographs and a same-session clean transcript, but no
  source video.
- Raw recorder exports, post-camp debriefs, private disclosures, and unrelated
  financial-hardship records are intentionally excluded.
